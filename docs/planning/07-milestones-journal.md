# Design: Milestones journal for Feeding Tracker

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-11-17
Branch: claude/focused-hamilton-47r7qx
Repo: Feeding-Tracker
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

The two parents want to record their baby's firsts (first smile, rolling over, first word) in the app they already open many times a day. Today a milestone is a photo on the phone, sent to family. What happened, the baby's age and the date get lost among thousands of other photos, and the moment is hard to find again months later.

The app may later be sold to other families. Feeding needs to be tracked for about 6 to 12 months, but milestones go on for years, so a journal also gives a family a reason to keep using the app after feeds settle.

## Demand Evidence

- Real for the app's two daily users: it is their own wish.
- Not yet proven for selling. No parent outside the household has asked for it or said they would pay.
- Rival apps sell milestone journals (Qeepsake, Tinybeans, The Days We Keep), usually with photos, prompts, family sharing and printed books, behind a subscription. So the need exists, but the field is crowded.
- Public reviews of baby apps name lost data as the most damaging complaint, then paywalls, then timelines of "expected ages" that worry parents and get early babies wrong.

## Status Quo

- Milestones live as photos and videos in the camera roll, often sent to family in a chat, with no note of what happened.
- The NHS red book has milestone pages that health visitors look at during checks. Many parents stop filling them in after the first months.
- What goes wrong: the photo has no words, the baby's age at the time is not recorded, and finding "when did she first roll over?" means scrolling through thousands of photos.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Target user:** the two parents, keeping one shared record on both phones. It builds on what the app already does best: two phones, one record, synced within seconds.
- **Narrowest wedge:** a Journal tab where a first, with a photo, is saved in under 30 seconds and found again by the baby's age. Up to 4 photos or short videos per first were added in the CEO review (D13).
- **Before building extras for selling:** show the journal to 3 to 5 other parents and write down their words.

## Constraints

- **The repository is public.** No real photos, names or dates go in code, tests, sample data or screenshots. Sample data uses drawings.
- **No third-party SDKs.** Everything stays in the household's iCloud, except what a parent exports or shares, and the copy of a photo or video taken in the app that is saved to the phone's Photos.
- **Facts only.** No expected ages, no "by now", no "should", no normal ranges, no red or green. Suggestions say what to note, never when it should happen.
- **The iCloud data layout only grows.** The `Media` record type is added to the live (Production) layout after TestFlight's first deploy, after the parents' go-ahead. TestFlight does not wait for it.
- **Photos use the household owner's iCloud storage.** If it fills up, sync stops for both phones.
- **Two phones, one record:** the same sync, field-by-field merge and Undo as feeds, sleep and nappies.
- **The look follows DESIGN.md**, day and night, and fits the iPhone Duo.
- **Photos are encrypted like every other detail.** Each photo and its thumbnail are stored with CloudKit encryption from their first import into the schema. This is checked against Apple's documentation for the chosen field type before any Production deploy. If CloudKit can't encrypt that field type, the engineering review chooses another way before any photo is uploaded.
- **The alarms count feeds only.** Adding, editing or deleting a milestone, or a photo arriving, never sets, moves or silences a feed alert. A FeedingCore test proves that milestone changes leave the planned alerts unchanged, as `06-more-than-feeds.md` does for sleep and nappies.

## Premises

All four agreed by the parents on 17 Nov 2026:

1. **Build it for the two parents first.** Every change is tried on them. Show 3 to 5 other parents before building extras for selling.
2. **Photos are a must.** The journal is built around photos, as a journal of words alone loses to the camera roll. One first may still be saved without a photo and given one later.
3. **Facts only, never expected ages.** Suggestions say what to note, never when.
4. **Selling is a later, separate decision.** This plan keeps that door open (no family data in code, a full export so nothing is ever lost) but does not build for the App Store yet.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: One more kind of entry, in History
A milestone is logged like a nappy, with one photo, and shows in History. Smallest change. Not chosen: it feels like a log line among nappies, not a keepsake.

### Approach B: A Journal tab, a photo timeline of firsts
A fourth tab with the baby's firsts, grouped by age, each with a photo. Chosen.

### Approach C: Capture now, write later
A one-tap "a moment happened" button everywhere, with the photo and words added later. Not chosen as the base. Its quick capture may come back as an addition.

## Recommended Approach

**B: a Journal tab.** A milestone is a keepsake, not a log line. It needs its own place, with photos, to beat the camera roll.

- **The tab:** a fourth tab beside Home, History and Graphs, laid out as "a line through time" (design review, D2: layout C). It has a big "Add a milestone" button (design review D5: a soft cream button pinned at the bottom, with the first's shape; never coral, so it can't be mistaken for Start feed) and a row of ideas (First laugh, Rolled over, First trip out, Something else…, where "Something else…" means typing your own). **Design review D6:** the ideas move into the Add screen, as suggestions under "What happened", and onto the Journal tab only while it is empty; the tab itself shows only its title, its top buttons and the firsts. "Something else…" goes: the Add button does that. Firsts are listed newest first and grouped by the baby's age ("8 weeks"). Each shows its first photo or video (with "+2" when it has more), title, date and who logged it.
- **One milestone:** its photos and videos large, swiped through in order, the baby's age and the date, the note, who logged it, Share and Edit (see "Rules the journal keeps"). **Design review D8 (section 2B of `journal-details.html`):** the photo in a rounded frame (`{rounded.xl}`, 26 pt) with the 16 pt gutter round it, swiped through with a "1 of 3" chip; then the title (Fredoka 26 pt), "6 weeks · 30 Oct 2026" (SF Pro Rounded, `{colors.text-muted}`), the note (body 17 pt) and "Logged by Sam" with the avatar; then Share and Edit as two soft buttons side by side. Delete is inside Edit. An item still coming shows the drawn tile with "On its way" in its place in the swipe.
- **Add a milestone:** (layout, design review D9, section 3A of `journal-details.html`: one form in a sheet; Cancel at the top left and Save at the top right; "What happened" with the ideas as suggestions under it; a row of 4 slots, the first labelled "Cover", a "+" slot offering "Choose from Photos" or "Take a photo or video", drag to reorder; When; Note.)
  - Pick an idea or type your own words.
  - Set when: now by default, or earlier. **From the photo (design review D17; engineering re-review eng2 D2):** if the first picked photo or video was taken more than 5 minutes before now (on any day), When uses its capture time and says so ("30 Oct, 07:40 · from the photo · Change"); with several items, the earliest; a photo taken just now, or in the app, keeps "now". The date is read before the location data is removed (D17 of the CEO review). The age refusals still apply. A FeedingCore test covers the choice of date; a device check confirms the system picker's file carries the date without full Photos permission.
  - Choose up to 4 photos or videos from the phone (the system photo picker), or take them in the app. A video longer than 15 seconds is trimmed with iOS's own trimmer before it is added. In Edit, items are put in order by dragging; the first one is the cover.
  - **Save never waits for videos (design review D16):** Save works at once; a slot whose video is still being made smaller shows a small progress ring and finishes in the background; if it fails, the first is kept and says "This video couldn't be added".
  - Add a note if you want, then Save. The title stops at 80 characters and the note at 5,000 (about 900 words); near each limit a count shows ("4,950 / 5,000") (engineering review, eng D5). As one character can hold many code points, the note is also held under 32 KB of text and the title under 1 KB, checked before saving, so no first is too big for iCloud (outside voice OE9).
  - **A photo is not required.** Save works with no photo, for example for a first word with no photo to hand. The list and the milestone's page then show a plain drawn tile in place of the photo. A photo can be added later through Edit.
- **Ideas, never ages.** The ideas row is a fixed list in a fixed order that is not by typical age. It never changes with the baby's age or with what has been logged. It never marks an idea as missing or "not yet". A logged idea stays in the row.
- **The baby's age** is worked out from the Baby record's birth date (see "Rules the journal keeps").
- **Wireframe:** `~/.gstack/projects/<owner>-Feeding-Tracker/<owner>-claude-focused-hamilton-47r7qx-sketch-20261117-222956.png` (the HTML is next to it). It stays out of the public repository. It was accepted as a starting shape; the look comes later, in the design review.

## Rules the journal keeps

**Words parents read (design review D32):** "first", never "milestone": "Add a first", a "Firsts" filter in History, "Delete this first?", "Sam logged a first", the "Firsts list". The tab is "Journal". In the UK, "milestones" is the red book's word for checks against expected ages, which this journal never shows. Code and this plan keep the word "milestone" for the record kind; every string a parent reads uses "first".

**Record shape.**
- A milestone is an `Event` of a new kind, `milestone`. Its words (title, note) go in the encrypted payload, with a raised `EventPayload` version for the new fields. So it gets the same field-by-field merge, Undo and soft delete as feeds, sleep and nappies.
- **Each photo or video is a `Media` record** of a new type, pointing at the milestone's ID. It holds the file and its thumbnail as CloudKit assets (CloudKit's file field). Apple has said that CloudKit stores asset data encrypted (WWDC21); the engineering review confirms this before any Production deploy. **Confirmed in the engineering review (18 Nov 2026):** Apple's "Encrypting user data" page says a `CKAsset` is encrypted by default, its key kept in an encrypted field, outside the public database (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/encrypting-user-data). So media are assets, and the fallback route below is not built.
  - If it is confirmed, media are assets, with no 1 MB limit.
  - If not, the fallback route: a photo is re-encoded until it fits under 900 KB as encrypted data in one record, and a video is split into encrypted parts under 900 KB, in records of their own (see "A hard size ceiling").
  - A new record type is a permitted addition to the iCloud layout.
  - The `Media` and allowance counter records are built in `RecordMapping`, beside `Event`, so the app test that checks every written field against `schema.ckdb` (`AppTests/RecordMappingTests.swift`) covers them (engineering review, 18 Nov 2026).
- **Up to 4 photos or short videos per first (CEO review, D13).** The milestone's payload names an ordered list of up to 4 media IDs, as one field that merges on its own. If both phones change the list at about the same time, field-by-field merge picks one list on both phones. **Changed by the parents' choice A (18 Nov 2026, after Codex's review of T3 and T4):** the two lists join instead: the order of the later change, then any item only the other list has, up to 4, so a photo added on each phone at once is kept on both; a photo leaves a first only when a parent takes it out (the removed list). An editor saves only what the parent changed, onto the first as it is now, so a photo that arrived while it was open stays. **Removed items stay removed (eng D10):** the payload also holds a list of removed media IDs that only grows; a merge joins both phones' lists, every media list leaves those IDs out, and the clean-up deletes only IDs on it. So an older list from a phone that was offline can never name a deleted item again.
- **Clean-up rule, one parent each:** a media record that the winning list doesn't name, or whose first was deleted, is deleted only by the parent whose iCloud account made it (the record's creator), so the same parent's new phone takes over after a reinstall. Records left by a parent who is no longer in the household are deleted by the owner's phone 30 days after they leave. It deletes it only once the change that left it out is older than the 10-second Undo window plus one minute, and it checks again just before deleting. So a media record that arrives before its first is never treated as unused, and Undo never brings back a list that names a deleted record. A restore that comes later drops the IDs of records already deleted from the list.
- Each item is a photo or a video of up to 15 seconds, in one `Media` record holding the file and its thumbnail on the asset route (for the fallback route, see "A hard size ceiling"). The first item is the one the timeline shows.
- **Photos live in a zone of their own** (`Photos`), not in the household zone.
  - The push-woken fetch, which the alarms depend on, fetches the household zone only. Both sync engines run with automatic sync on, which fetches every zone by default (`App/Sync/HouseholdSync.swift:77`), so neither sync engine ever fetches the photos zone; only the app's own media requests do. It is left out of every engine fetch: the automatic fetches (the delegate's `nextFetchChangesOptions`), the push path (`handlePush` → `fetchNow`, `HouseholdSync.swift:315-326`) and the join step (`HouseholdSync.swift:198`). A test of the push path proves it. The photos zone's name is defined once (in `AppConfig`), and every engine fetch goes through one `HouseholdSync` helper that applies `.allExcluding(...)` (`CKSyncEngine.FetchChangesOptions.Scope`, iOS 17 and later), so a fetch added later can't forget it (engineering review, 18 Nov 2026). The helper builds the zone ID for each engine and role: the owner's private engine leaves out the owner's own photos zone (`CKCurrentUserDefaultName`); the member's shared engine leaves out the photos zone under the owner's name, as `writeTarget` does for the household zone (`HouseholdSync.swift:109-121`). Its test covers both roles and the automatic, push and join fetches (outside voice OE1).
  - The photos zone is read only by the app's own media requests, when the app is open or in a later background task.
  - **Who creates it:** only the household owner's phone can create a zone in the owner's iCloud. The owner's phone creates the photos zone and its share the first time a build with the journal starts, not when the Journal is first opened. The household share allows no access by link alone (`publicPermission = .none`, `HouseholdSync.swift:174`), so the owner's phone first adds the other parent to the new share as a participant, found by their iCloud user identity from the household share. It then writes the share's link into a household setting, and the other parent's phone accepts it by itself, with no second invite to tap. Whenever the household share gains a participant later (a parent who joins later, or is invited again after the shares ended), the owner's phone adds them to the photos share in the same way, making a new one if the old one ended, and writes the new link. The engineering review confirms this. If it can't be automatic, the link travels as the household invite does, in Messages.
  - Until the other phone has accepted, it logs firsts and keeps their photos and videos on the phone, marked "not yet backed up", and uploads them once it can.
  - If the background fetch can't leave the zone out, the engineering review measures on the two phones that a push fetch carrying media still applies feeds and runs `reconcile` within the background time, and that feeds arrive as fast as before (outside review, O3).
  - **Older builds** skip record types they don't know (`App/Sync/RecordMapping.swift:111`), so media change nothing on them. An older build has no rule leaving the photos zone out, so its fetch after a background wake-up would download media in the time the alarms rely on. So once the photos zone exists, the owner's phone must not go back to an older build. The two-phone timing check includes the owner's phone on the journal build fetching after a wake-up.

**Where milestones show.**
- The Journal tab, and History, under All and a new "Milestones" filter, as one line with the title and no photo. **Design review D32 and D43:** the filter reads "Firsts"; the row is a plain `{colors.surface}` row with the sparkle and the time on the left, the title in the middle and the logging parent's initial on the right; a tap opens the first's page. History's swipe Delete (`App/Home/HistoryView.swift:131-133`, which deletes at once today) uses the Journal's delete for a milestone, with its confirmation and Undo (outside review, O9).
- Never on Home's tiles, the graphs, the daily rhythm report, the 24-hour summary, the widgets or the watch, and never in the alerts.

**Photos never get in the way of feeds.**
- Photo uploads and downloads never go ahead of event records, or in the same batch.
- Media upload and download outside the sync engine, one record at a time. Uploads go only when no feed or other event is waiting to upload, so a feed logged during a video upload goes first. An item already sending must not delay a feed either (outside review, O3). **How (eng D3):** media requests run at a lower priority than event sends, and if a feed or any other entry has waited 5 seconds behind a photo or video in flight, that transfer is stopped and sent again from the start afterwards. Proven by the two-phone check on mobile data and a unit test of the 5-second rule. Downloads use their own requests, so a feed arriving through the sync engine never waits behind a video. **And step aside (eng D9):** whenever an engine fetch starts (after a push, on opening, or on joining), any photo or video download in flight stops at once and starts again from the beginning when the fetch is done.
- When a push wakes the app in the background, it applies the new records and runs `reconcile` before any photo download.
- Photo transfers may wait for the app to be open, or for a later background task.
- Two-phone device checks, on Wi-Fi and on mobile data, with the receiving phone locked (as spike 2 measured; each media upload also sends that phone a silent push): a feed logged while a photo, and then a video, is uploading reaches the other phone as fast as before; and a feed arrives as fast as before while this phone is downloading a video.

**Photos never fill iCloud by surprise.**
- If the household owner's iCloud fills up for any reason, every upload stops, feeds included. The app already warns: "Feeds stay on this phone until there is space." Photos make that far more likely, so a guard acts before iCloud is full.
- **A household photo allowance**, counted by the app itself. Each `Media` record stores its own size in bytes as a plain field, as CloudKit doesn't report a record's size. **One shared counter (CEO review, D20):** one counter record in the photos zone holds the bytes used. Before each upload, a phone adds the item's size to it; iCloud saves the counter only if nobody changed it since the phone read it, and on a clash the phone reads it again and retries. Only an upload whose size fits under the limit goes ahead, so the limit holds even when both phones upload at once after time offline (each phone counting only what it had seen could go over by a whole offline backlog; outside review, O7). The maker's clean-up lowers the counter when it deletes an item. **The counter lists each item (engineering review, eng D2):** the record lists each item (its media ID) and its size, and the bytes used are their sum. **Saved together (eng D11):** an item's entry is written in the same all-or-nothing save as its `Media` record, not before the upload; on a clash both fail, and the phone reads the counter again, checks the limit and saves again. The clean-up deletes a `Media` record and its entry in one all-or-nothing save. So no entry can be left without its item, and no item without its entry (this replaces the "before each upload" reservation above and eng D2's 7-day give-back). About 50 bytes per item (about 60 KB at 1,200 items), well under CloudKit's 1 MB record limit. The counter is a new record type, a permitted addition to `schema.ckdb`. The allowance starts at 1 GB (D14). The limit is one shared household setting, not one per phone. Settings shows how much is used, and either parent can raise the limit there.
- At 80% of the allowance, both phones say so. When it is reached, new photo and video uploads pause, and the milestones' words still sync. Paused items stay on the phone, marked "not yet backed up", and go into the export. The parents can raise the allowance or delete items to go on.
- The copy saved to the phone's Photos counts against that parent's own iCloud Photos, not the household allowance. Settings says so in one line.
- Each photo is scaled to at most 2048 pixels on its long side and compressed. Its thumbnail is at most 400 pixels. A Live Photo is added as its still photo; a HEIC photo is converted in the same step; a screen recording is a video and follows the video rules (engineering review).
- **No location data (CEO review, D17).** In the same step, each photo and video loses its location data (where it was taken). So no copy the journal makes, in iCloud, in the export or in Share, carries it. The originals in the phone's Photos are unchanged. A test proves an added photo and video carry none.
- **Each video** is cut to 15 seconds at most, and turned on the phone into a smaller copy, aiming at 720p and under 8 MB, with sound. Its thumbnail is a still frame of at most 400 pixels.
- Videos count towards the household photo allowance. Settings shows how much is used, so the parents can see that a video uses many times the space of a photo.
- **A hard size ceiling (the fallback route only, if CloudKit can't encrypt assets):** not built, as assets are encrypted (engineering review, 18 Nov 2026); kept here for the record.
  - A photo is re-encoded at lower quality, then at a smaller size, until it fits under 900 KB as encrypted data in one record.
  - A video is split into encrypted parts under 900 KB each, in records of their own.
  - On either route, an item is never left half-saved.

**Sync promise.**
- The words reach the other phone within seconds, like a feed.
- A photo or video arrives within a minute of the other phone's app being open on Wi-Fi. Previews and full items download at once on any network, mobile data included (engineering review, eng D7). While the app is in the background, it may come later, when iOS runs a background task.
- To be measured on the two phones.
- While a photo or video is still coming, its tile shows the plain drawing with "On its way".
- **Three looks, one meaning each (design review D10):** no photo: the drawn tile with the first's shape; coming: the drawn tile with a small progress ring and "On its way" (a tap tries again; "Not here yet" is merged into it); on this phone only: a small cloud mark and "Only on this iPhone" beside the date (it replaces "not yet backed up"). A tap on any mark says why and what to do, for example "The photo allowance is full: raise it in Settings" or "No connection: it will go when you're online".
- Tiles use the 400-pixel thumbnail, fetched on its own (the request names only the thumbnail and small fields, `desiredKeys`), so the timeline never downloads full files; the milestone page decodes photos at screen size (engineering review).
- If a download fails, the tile says "Not here yet" and tries again later, and again when tapped.

**Where photos live on the phone.**
- Photos, videos and thumbnails are files of their own in the app's container, with file protection, named by the item's ID. They are never stored inside `household.json`.
- A deleted milestone's or a removed item's files are removed from the phone.
- **Space kept free for feeds (CEO review, D19).** Before adding a video, downloading items or making an export, the app checks the phone's free space and keeps a reserve (for example 500 MB; the engineering review sets the number), so feeds can always be saved. Below it, the app says "Not enough space on this iPhone" and waits; a download waits and its tile says "Not here yet". Temporary files from Share and Export are deleted when done. A test proves feeds still save when the phone is nearly full of journal items. An export is written as a folder and then packed into one file. Its check counts the items it still has to download, plus the packed file, plus the reserve; the folder is made of APFS copies that share storage with the originals (clones), so it adds almost nothing. A half-made export left by an app that was stopped is deleted at the next launch (engineering review; outside voice OE8).
- The widgets and the watch never load photos.

**No photo is lost.**
- A photo or video taken in the app is also saved to the phone's Photos. This needs the add-only photo permission. **When the app asks (design review D20):** the first tap on "Take a photo or video" shows one short app screen about the camera and microphone, then iPhone asks; the add-only Photos permission is asked after Save, not before, so the moment is caught first. Choosing from Photos needs no permission.
- Until an item has uploaded, its milestone shows a small "not yet backed up" mark. **Design review D10 and D11:** the mark reads "Only on this iPhone", and it appears only if the item is still not in iCloud 10 minutes after Save, or at once when sending has failed (no connection, the allowance full, an iCloud error).
- If a parent declines the add-only permission, the first is still saved. It keeps the "not yet backed up" mark until it uploads, and the app says once that it was not copied to Photos.
- Taking a video in the app needs the camera and microphone permissions. If the microphone is declined, the app takes photos only and says once how to turn the microphone on in Settings; videos can still be chosen from the phone.

**Deleting.**
- Delete asks to confirm. The milestone's words are hidden, like any deleted entry, so the other phone learns of the delete.
- Its `Media` records are truly deleted from iCloud and from both phones, unlike feeds, by the clean-up rule in "Record shape". Removing or replacing an item deletes the old one in the same way.
- The true deletion happens only after the 10-second Undo window has passed, so Undo brings the photo back. A milestone's delete and an item's removal wait on this phone until their Undo window ends before they are sent, as a new entry does (`uploadNotBefore`), so an Undo can never reach the maker's phone after its clean-up (outside review, O6). The hold is also checked when the sync engine asks for the record to send (`recordToSend`, `App/Sync/HouseholdSync.swift:300`, which checks only `needsUpload` today), so a change queued earlier can't carry a held delete out early; one `HouseholdData` rule answers "may this go now?" for both paths. Going to the background ends the hold and the Undo together (`endUndoWindows`, `HouseholdData.swift:218`; Undo checks the same deadline at `:212`), as for a new entry today (engineering outside voice OE4). Removing one item from a first works the same way.
- After that, bringing back a deleted milestone brings back its words only. The delete confirmation says so: "Its photos and videos will be removed from both phones for good." **Design review D15:** the confirmation ends "…for good once Undo ends". The Undo bar (`UndoBanner`, without "Change time" for a first) reads "First smile added · Undo" after Save; after Delete, from the first's page or from History, the screen goes back to the Journal or History and shows "First smile deleted · Undo" just above the bottom button; after removing an item in Edit and saving, "Photo removed · Undo". VoiceOver reads the bar out.

**Delete all data and leaving the household.** "Delete all data" is in the plan but not yet built.
- When it is built, it warns that the journal and its photos will go, gives their count, and offers the export first.
- Ending the sharing, or removing the other parent, ends both shares: the household share and the photos share. Apple's invite sheet can remove a parent without telling the app (its delegate reports only save failures, `App/Sync/HouseholdSync.swift:542-556`). So on each launch and sync the owner's phone checks the photos share against the household share and removes anyone no longer in it; a removal that didn't finish is tried again (outside review, O4). The invite sheet runs inside the app, so its "share saved" and "stopped sharing" callbacks start this check at once; while a removal is pending, Settings shows "Removing access to photos" (engineering outside voice OE7).
- If the shares end, the household owner keeps everything. The phone of the parent who leaves keeps its local journal (the words, and the photos it already holds), as it keeps feeds today, and can still export from it. Photos and videos it never downloaded can't be fetched after the shares end. The same rules cover both zones.

**Export ships with the first Journal build.**
- "Export the journal" makes one file to save or AirDrop: every photo and video, named by date and title, plus a list of each first with its date, the baby's age and the note.
- It is the parents' own copy, so nothing depends on iCloud alone.
- The export screen says: "This file holds every photo and video. Keep it somewhere private." (D17). Its items carry no location data.
- **It keeps going in the background (engineering review, eng D4).** The Export tap starts an iOS continued-processing task (`BGContinuedProcessingTaskRequest`, iOS 26 and later), so the export carries on if the parent locks the phone or switches app, with iOS's own progress display. If iOS ends it early, the half-made file is deleted and the app says "Export stopped: open the app to finish". **The screen, start to finish (design review D12; its wording replaces "open the app to finish", which wrongly implied the deleted file could carry on):** the privacy note and an Export button; then "Getting photos: 12 of 48", then "Packing the file", with a progress bar and Cancel; then "Save the file", the file kept until this screen closes, so closing the share sheet loses nothing; if items are missing, their names with "Save without these 3" or "Try again"; if iOS stopped it, a notification and, on this screen, "The export stopped before it finished" with "Try again".
- Before it writes the file, the export fetches every photo this phone does not yet hold, showing progress.
- If any photo can't be fetched (offline, or an iCloud error), the export names the missing ones and does not call itself complete.

**Share is in the first build (CEO review, D11 and D15, 17 Nov 2026).** A Share button on a first sends it by the iPhone's share sheet.
- **For a photo, the app draws a card (D15):** the photo with the first's title and the baby's age on it, in the app's look. The card is a new picture, so it carries no location data. Its look is decided in the design review. **Design review D25 (section 4A of `journal-details.html`):** a 4:5 card (1080 × 1350 px), always in the day look: the photo fills the top (the whole photo fitted, no face cropped where it can be avoided); a solid `{colors.background}` band below holds the first's shape in `{colors.text-muted}`, the title in Fredoka (wrapping, the band grows for a long title) and the age in SF Pro Rounded. Nothing is written on the photo; no baby's name, app name or location. With no birth date, the age line is left out.

If that happens at once, undoing a replace or restoring a deleted milestone brings back the words without the photo, while the screen still offers Undo. The document does not say which promise gives way.
7. R2-7 — completeness (blocking): The export promises every photo, but the design allows a phone to lack some. Photo downloads may wait for the app to be open or for a later background task, a failed download shows 'Photo not here yet', and a photo just added on the other phone may not have arrived. An export made at such a moment would silently leave those photos out while looking complete. That defeats its purpose as the parents' own copy, so that 'nothing depends on iCloud alone'. The same applies to the export offered to a parent who leaves the household.
8. R2-8 — feasibility (blocking): 'The parent who leaves is offered the export before their phone drops the household's data, as it does for feeds' describes behavior the app does not have. When the shared zone goes, App/Sync/HouseholdSync.swift keeps everything ('Round 2, S9: keep everything on this phone and say so. Never delete anything here.') and shows 'The shared household is gone from iCloud. Feeds and alerts on this phone are kept.' Also, 02-plan-v1.md keeps the last alerts when data goes missing after a share is revoked. Feeds are therefore not dropped. The design either invents a new deletion for the journal, against the keep-everything rule, or misstates what happens. What the leaving parent's phone keeps of the journal, and when it can still export, is unclear.
9. R2-9 — consistency (blocking): The Sync promise and the Success Criteria say that the photo reaches the other phone within a minute on Wi-Fi. But 'Photos never get in the way of feeds' lets photo transfers wait for the app to be open or for a later background task, which iOS may run hours later. On a locked phone with the app in the background, the usual case, both rules cannot hold, and the planned measurement on the two phones has no defined starting point.
10. R2-15 — completeness (blocking): The refusal works only when a milestone is entered. The design also lets milestones be logged before any birth date exists (grouped by calendar month), and adds a Settings row that sets or corrects the birth date and so recomputes every age. A birth date set or corrected later can fall after existing milestones: a wrong year, or a first logged before the birth date was entered. The design does not say what those milestones show, or whether such a birth date is refused.

- **New components** (names for the engineering review): in FeedingCore, the milestone payload fields, the baby's age, the media-list merge field, the clean-up decision, the allowance decision and the Firsts list; in the app, the Journal tab, the milestone editor and page, the Share card drawing, the export builder, `MediaStore`, `MediaTransfer` and `PhotosZoneManager`; in `schema.ckdb`, the `Media` record type and the allowance counter record type (D20).
- **Data flow, four paths (a media item):** happy: picked or taken → scaled → saved as a file → listed in the milestone → uploaded after feeds → fetched by the other phone's own request. Nil: a first with no media saves its words only. Empty: a zero-byte or unreadable file is refused with "This photo couldn't be added". Error: upload fails → quota: pause and warn; network: retry with backoff; too large: re-encode (fallback route).
- **State machine, a media item:**
```
  [on this phone only] --upload ok--> [backed up] --removed--> [removal pending (Undo window + 1 min)] --> [deleted]
        |   ^                                ^                         |
        |   +--retry (network)--+            +---------Undo------------+
        +--allowance reached--> [paused] --limit raised / space--> [on this phone only]
  invalid: [backed up] -> [on this phone only]; [deleted] -> anything (a restore drops the ID instead)
```
- **State machine, the photos share:** none → zone made (owner) → other parent added → link written → accepted (other phone). Each step that fails is retried at the next launch and on the next sync; the other phone keeps items "not yet backed up" meanwhile.
- **Coupling:** `MediaTransfer` talks to CloudKit outside the sync engines. This is new coupling, justified because it keeps media behind feeds. Its upload queue must survive the app being closed: **required (invariant: no photo is lost)**.
- **Scale:** at 10 times the expected use (about 300 firsts and 1,200 items), the timeline needs thumbnails loaded lazily; the allowance, not the code, is the limit.
- **Single point of failure:** the owner's phone, for making the photos zone and adding a parent. Stated in the plan; the other phone degrades to "not yet backed up".
- **Rollback:** the `Media` type is permanent once in Production but harmless. As the owner's phone must not go back to an older build, the rollback is a newer build with the Journal hidden: about a day through TestFlight.
- **Accepted additions:** X1 and X2 fit cleanly. X4's card is a self-contained drawing. X6 adds the most moving parts (an item list, video conversion, video storage). The build order (step 2) contains the risk.

- **Async ordering, invariant "a live item is never deleted":** schedule A: phone B removes an item, phone A's clean-up runs after the Undo window plus a minute, B's Undo arrives later → the restore drops the dead ID (no broken list). Regression proof: a FeedingCore test of the clean-up decision with both orders, using fixed times.
- **Async ordering, invariant "feeds never wait for media":** an upload in progress and a feed logged: `MediaTransfer` checks for waiting events before each media record and yields; downloads use their own requests.
